# Traffic and police

Through traffic and police live in `src/vehicle/traffic.ts`. The session attaches a `TrafficState` to `town.traffic` after it builds the town (`Game.attachTraffic`). Test maps, the tower test, demos, the brick job, and headless sim tests get no traffic unless they create it.

## Edge roads

`src/world/edgeRoads.ts` runs at most one straight public road per side from the town out to the map bounds. It starts from the closest point on an existing public road whose run to the edge misses lots, buildings, open spaces and other roads. Rural and campaign layouts compute their bounds before adding edge roads, then keep those bounds. The estate does the same. Roadside dressing may sit on the shoulder but not in a public travel lane (`propInTravelLane` in `rural.ts`).

An exit is any public road end within 1.5 units of the bounds (`edgeExitNodes`). Each exit has one inbound and one outbound lane.

## Civilians

- **Density:** set per level in `DENSITY`: County 6, Village 8, Township 12, Suburb 14, City Borough 22, City Downtown 28, Governor's Mansion 6. About 60% of the cap is placed on lanes at the start; the rest enter from the edges. A car does not enter while another is within 26 units of that exit and heading for it, or while the dozer is within 16.
- **Speed:** `CRUISE` by road class, scaled up from posted limits so a car outruns the 8.6 u/s dozer (residential 9.5, rural 12, arterial 12.5). Cars slow for bends ahead and stay slow until they are square in their lane again.
- **Routing:** Dijkstra over lane links. Costs are travel seconds plus penalties for U-turns, driveways, lanes blocked by rubble, wrecks or props (refreshed once a second), and lanes passing within 7 units of something the driver had to turn back from in the last 8 s.
- **Line through a turn:** lanes meet at the middle of a junction, which would make every turn a hook. `setPath` drops two lane points each side of a turn so the route is a corner a car can follow, and moves every point up to 0.3 units toward the car's own kerb, because lanes sit close to the centre line.
- **Following:** `pathGap` walks the path ahead against every vehicle, the dozer and on-road props. The car holds a speed it could stop from if the one ahead braked hard, and settles at a time gap behind moving traffic. A car coming the other way counts only if the two would touch. A car off its line (coming out of a turn) is tested along the ground it will actually sweep.
- **Junctions:** three or more roads meeting form a junction. Junction nodes within 6 units share one set of claims. A car claims the junction when no claim's line across it comes within 1.2 units of its own, so opposing through traffic and turns that never meet go together; cars following in from the same road join for up to 5 s, then the side road gets its turn. A car standing in the junction without a claim closes only the lines that run over it.
- **Keeping junctions clear:** a car takes a junction only if it can drive out the far side: a stopped queue beyond it means waiting at the line. Junctions too close together to stop between are claimed as one. A car stopped short of a junction gives its claim up, and a claim ends as soon as the car is out of the box.
- **Lock-ups:** two cars each in the other's way: the older eases past at once and the newer waits, then backs off. Cars stopped nose to nose from opposite directions (a narrow road, a junction mouth) both squeeze by. Ten seconds stuck behind traffic also eases past. A car pressing forward without moving reverses briefly and replans. Only after four back-offs against a head-on car does a driver turn around.
- **The dozer:** drivers have no long-range sight of it. It matters in two cases. *In the road ahead*: it is an obstacle on the path (its box runs to the blade edge, and a moving dozer also blocks the spot it will reach in 0.6 s), so the car brakes and stops about 2.4 units short. *Right beside the car* (within 2.6 units): the car eases to 4 u/s and leans up to 0.6 units away while the pavement allows. A dozer behind the tail or out in the lots is ignored.
- **After braking:** if the dozer is driving at the car, the car reverses while the road behind is clear and three-point-turns after 1.2 s. If it has stopped, the car waits 0.6 s and looks for a way round (`passOffset`): a line 0.75 units clear of the obstacle that stays on the pavement, misses everything else and has nobody oncoming. It backs up first when too close to swing out, passes at 3.5 u/s, and returns to its lane. With no way round for 1.6 s it three-point-turns and routes away. Wrecks and props in the lane get the same treatment on a slower clock (1.2 s, then a reroute, then a turn at 4.5 s). Stopped traffic is waited out and is never a reason to turn back.
- **Three-point turn:** forward lock, reverse opposite lock, then away on the opposite lane. Each swing ends early at the road edge and holds for anything in the way; a turn boxed in for 8 s is abandoned. Civilians never leave the pavement.
- **Exit:** cars leave the map at an exit. A car stuck for 30 s far from the dozer is removed.
## Police

- **Dispatch (Code Enforcement):** Time Challenge only, and only once the run earns it. `TrafficState.alert` starts at 0. `Game.judgeCampaign` calls `raiseEnforcement`: level 1 on the landmark's first breach or half the dollar target, level 2 when the landmark is down or the demolition spree meter passes `SPREE_BACKUP`. The cap is two cruisers on every level, the alert never drops, a cruiser arrives `policeDelay` (4 s) after the alert, and a replacement 14 s after one goes down. In Sandbox, **P** queues one cruiser, recorded as a replay action.
- **Sight:** `SIGHT` is the cruiser's view for a straight charge: a 30-unit, ±55° cone from the front bumper, blocked by standing ground- and first-floor cells (`sightBlocked`).
- **Pursuit:** police always know where the dozer is. They route at 16 u/s with light penalties and pass traffic by swerving around it. They charge directly (off-road if needed) when `policeMayLeaveRoad` from `src/world/routing.ts` allows it: within 22 units, past any ram hold-off, and not blocked. Blocked means no line of sight beyond 9 units, or rubble over 0.5 high on the straight line between them (`rubbleBetween`; the last 3 units by the dozer are ignored). Otherwise they keep to the lanes, where buried lanes cost extra.
- **Bounty:** a wrecked cruiser pays `CRUISER_BOUNTY` ($150) into run escrow, counts as a wrecked vehicle for the Shaped-Charge Blade's refund, and adds to the career's `lifetimeCruisersWrecked` at settlement.
- **Hits:** the cruiser's nose is tested against the dozer's full footprint, blade included. Any touch ends the charge and sends the cruiser back to reverse. A collision with more than 2 u/s closing speed counts as one hit, from the cruiser ramming, the dozer ramming, or the blade at speed. Each hit folds the cruiser's nose in by a third; three wreck it (`wreckAfterHits`) and pay its bounty.
- **Ram cost to the dozer:** heat `10 + 1.1·force` and track `7 + 0.8·force`, with force capped at 12. Both cool quickly, so one cruiser stings and two at once hurt. The dozer also takes a small velocity jolt.
- **Push resistance:** the dozer's push mass is `8 × hit × (blade down ? hit : 1) × (1 + 0.5·speed/max)`, where `hit` is 1, or JATO's ×5 during a burn, (`dozerPushMass` in `vehicle/world.ts`). It decides how much each side gives in any dozer-vehicle contact, so the 5-mass cruiser shoves harder than a 2-mass sedan unless the dozer is under JATO, blade down, or moving. A crushed wreck counts for up to 3.5× its mass (`pushMass`), so the flattened cruiser is the hardest thing on the road to move.

## Tests

`src/vehicle/traffic.test.ts` covers density by map, free flow through exits without traffic crashes, a busy suburb draining for 90 s with nobody stuck or wrecked, braking for a parked dozer and going around it or turning back without touching it, ignoring a dozer behind or off to the side, the police sight cone and building occlusion, police pursuit and the three-hit wreck, and the sandbox request. `src/world/edgeRoads.test.ts` checks that every campaign level has at least two exits and that every entrance reaches another exit.
